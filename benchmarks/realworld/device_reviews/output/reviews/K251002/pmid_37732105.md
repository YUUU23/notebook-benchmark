## Clinical Context

The tool addresses the diagnosis of periodontal diseases by measuring and visualizing bone levels associated with each tooth from radiographic images and is proposed as a diagnostic assist tool for clinicians.

## Experimental Set-up

The paper does not provide specific details about whether the study was retrospective or prospective, the exact patient population, or the time period of the study.

## Metrics

The paper reports that the AI model achieves high sensitivity and specificity, but does not provide exact values or confidence intervals.

## Comparison to Baselines

No explicit baseline comparators such as human performance or standard of care are discussed in the paper.

---

## Critique: Clinical Validity

The clinical validity of improving diagnosis of periodontal disease is meaningful, but without details on the population or the specifics of the disease spectrum, it's unclear if the deployment will handle a typical clinical range of cases.

## Critique: Baselines and Metrics

There is a lack of a clear baseline for comparison. The paper states high sensitivity and specificity, but without comparison to current diagnostic standards or human readers, the context of these improvements is unclear.

## Critique: Statistics

Statistical methods and sample size are not discussed in the paper, and without this information, it is difficult to assess the robustness of the results.

---

## Serious Issues

- No independent test set mentioned, raising concerns about generalizability of the results.
- Lack of detailed baseline comparisons against standard diagnostic procedures or human expert performance.
- Missing detailed statistics and confidence intervals for reported metrics.

## Minor Issues

- The study does not specify demographic or geographic diversity, which may limit generalizability.
- Impact factor of the journal is unknown, which affects the perceived credibility of the publication.
